CXO Demo — Anticipated Questions & Answers

Prepared responses for the most likely questions from leadership during the Capillary 360 funding pitch.

💰 ROI & Business Value

CXOs will want hard numbers and business justification

What's the ROI? How do we justify the investment?
Answer: Three measurable returns:
  • Time savings: BRD creation goes from 3-5 days to 15 minutes. Gap analysis from 2-3 days to always-current. That's 60-70% of SA/PM time freed up.
  • Revenue protection: Real-time sentiment detection catches at-risk accounts weeks before escalation. One saved enterprise renewal pays for the platform.
  • Revenue growth: Continuous gap analysis surfaces upsell opportunities automatically. Sales gets "Client X uses 40% of licensed modules" alerts without asking.

Conservative estimate: if we save each SA 15 hours/week and enable 3x more project throughput, the platform pays for itself within one quarter.

Show the live agent generating a BRD assessment in under 60 seconds vs. the manual process.
What does this cost to run? What's the AI spend?
Answer: We've engineered aggressive cost optimization:
  • Dual-model architecture: Expensive reasoning model (Sonnet) only for complex analysis. Fast model (Haiku) for 80% of tasks at ~50x lower cost.
  • Parallel research agents: 60-70% cheaper than sequential Sonnet calls.
  • Off-topic gate: Blocks irrelevant requests before they hit the expensive model.
  • Context compaction: Keeps token usage lean on long conversations.

Current production cost: approximately $0.15-0.50 per complex assessment. Document generation (SDD/BRD): $1-3 per document. Compare to 4-8 hours of SA time at loaded cost.

Show the LangSmith trace with cost breakdown for a real request.
How does this impact headcount? Are we replacing people?
Answer: No. We're multiplying capacity, not replacing people. The platform handles the repetitive information gathering and document maintenance that consumes 60-70% of SA/PM time today. This frees them to focus on what humans do best: client relationships, creative problem-solving, and strategic decisions.

The goal is 3x more projects per SA — not fewer SAs. We're capacity-constrained today, not headcount-heavy.

Never frame this as "replacing" anyone. Frame as "enabling the team to handle 3x the workload without burning out."

🛡️ Trust, Safety & Accuracy

The #1 concern for CXOs — can we trust AI output?

How do we know the AI isn't hallucinating? What if it gives wrong information to a client?
Answer: Multiple layers of protection:
  • Research-first: The agent NEVER guesses. It searches Jira, Confluence, and product docs before every answer. Every claim is backed by a source link.
  • Validation node: Every response passes through a quality check that verifies sources exist and conclusions follow from evidence.
  • Human-in-the-loop: No document is published, no action is taken without human approval. The AI drafts — humans decide.
  • Confidence flagging: When the agent isn't sure, it explicitly says so and flags for SA review rather than guessing.
During demo, ask the agent something it can't find in the sources. Show how it responds with "No precedent found" rather than making something up.
What about data security? Is client data going to OpenAI/Anthropic?
Answer:
  • Enterprise API: We use Anthropic's enterprise API with zero data retention — they don't train on our data, don't store it, don't log it.
  • Data stays in our infra: MongoDB Atlas (SOC 2 compliant), Railway deployment (our control), all within our security perimeter.
  • User isolation: Every user only sees their own conversations. Role-based access at the database level.
  • Audit trail: Every action logged with timestamps and actor identification.
Show the admin panel with audit logs. Mention Anthropic's enterprise data policy explicitly.
What happens when the AI makes a mistake? Who's accountable?
Answer: The AI is a tool, not a decision-maker. Every output goes through human review before it reaches a client or becomes official. The accountability chain is unchanged — the SA/PM who approves the output owns it, just like they own a document they wrote manually.

The difference: they're reviewing a well-researched draft instead of starting from scratch. The quality bar is the same — the speed is 10x faster.

🎯 Strategic & Competitive

Why now? Why us? What's the moat?

Why build this ourselves? Can't we just buy a tool?
Answer: No off-the-shelf tool has:
  • Capillary domain knowledge: Our agent knows Loyalty+, Engage+, Neo, Connect+ — what's OOTB, what needs config, what needs custom dev. No generic tool has this.
  • Our data access: It searches our Jira, our Confluence, our product docs. A generic tool can't do this without massive integration work anyway.
  • Our process knowledge: The BRD pipeline, guardrail system, gate methodology — this is our IP, encoded as agent skills.

Generic AI tools (Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise) are horizontal. We need a vertical solution that understands Capillary's products, processes, and clients. That's what we're building.

What if AI models get cheaper/better? Does this become obsolete?
Answer: Models getting better is tailwind, not headwind. Our architecture is model-agnostic — we can swap Claude for GPT-5 or Gemini or open-source models without changing the platform. The value isn't in the model — it's in:
  • The Impact Dependency Graph (our IP)
  • The skill library (our domain knowledge encoded)
  • The data connections (Jira, Confluence, codebase)
  • The process automation (BRD pipeline, gates, guardrails)

Better models = our platform gets smarter automatically. We ride the wave, we don't fight it.

What's the competitive advantage for Capillary? Can this be client-facing?
Answer: Two angles:
  • Internal moat: Faster delivery, better client experience, proactive issue detection. Clients see faster turnaround and more proactive communication — without knowing AI is behind it.
  • Future product opportunity: The Consumer Agent layer could eventually become a client-facing product — imagine clients having their own "Capillary Assistant" that answers questions about their integration, suggests optimizations, and surfaces insights from their data.
Don't promise client-facing product in this pitch. Keep it as "future possibility" — the immediate ask is internal efficiency.

👥 Adoption & Change Management

Will people actually use this?

How do we get the team to actually adopt this?
Answer: The current agent is already in daily use by the PS team — adoption isn't theoretical, it's proven. The key design principles:
  • Zero friction: Ask a question in natural language, get an answer. No training needed.
  • Meets them where they are: Nudges via Slack and email — no new dashboard to check.
  • Immediate value: First use saves 30+ minutes. People come back because it works.
  • Doesn't replace workflow: Augments existing tools (Jira, Confluence) rather than replacing them.
Show usage stats from the current agent if available. Real adoption numbers are the strongest argument.
What if the quality isn't good enough and people stop trusting it?
Answer: Quality is enforced architecturally, not hoped for:
  • Validation node: Every output is checked before delivery
  • Guardrail system: BRDs must pass G1-G6 checks, all sections scored ≥ 3/5
  • Feedback loop: When users reject or correct outputs, the system learns (skill updates, prompt refinements)
  • Observability: We monitor response quality metrics and can catch degradation early

The gate system means bad outputs literally cannot proceed to the next stage. Quality is a hard constraint, not a soft goal.
